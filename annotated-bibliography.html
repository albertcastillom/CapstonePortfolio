Learning by doing

Annotated Bibliography

Theme 01

Apple Screen Time Frameworks

I used Apple’s official documentation to understand the capabilities, permissions, and privacy limits, and implementation of FamilyControls, ManagedSettings, and DeviceActivity. I paired that reference with practical implementation guides so I could connect the frameworks, persist app selections, schedule focus sessions, and apply or remove shields. This combination of official documentation and working examples guided the app’s defining feature and confirmed that a native Swift implementation was the right direction.

  • Apple. “Screen Time Technology Frameworks.” Apple Developer Documentation, developer.apple.com/documentation/screentimeapidocumentation. Accessed 19 Aug. 2026.

  • Brussee, Julius. “A Developer’s Guide to Apple’s Screen Time APIs (FamilyControls, ManagedSettings, DeviceActivity).” Medium, 23 Apr. 2025, medium.com/@juliusbrussee/a-developers-guide-to-apple-s-screen-time-apis-familycontrols-managedsettings-deviceactivity-e660147367d7.

  • Ustunel, Ezgi. “Screen Time API.” iOS Nest, Medium, 13 Oct. 2024, medium.com/ios-nest/screen-time-api-d1110751d2ce.

Theme 02

Architecture and React Native Exploration

I began the project by learning React Native and Expo and by studying how the frontend, authentication, database, and other parts of application fit together, since it was my first mobile app. The broader architecture resources helped me compare technology choices and identify Supabase for authentication and data storage, but also determine what is standard. When I investigated bridging Apple’s native Screen Time frameworks through an Expo module, the added complexity helped me decide to pivot from the cross-platform prototype to a fully native Swift and SwiftUI app.

  • Expo. “How to Create a Native Module with the Expo Modules API.” YouTube, 6 Sep. 2024, www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdaQSlyGik8&t=885s.

  • JavaScript Mastery. “SaaS App Full Course 2026 | Launch Your SaaS in Under 7 Days with Next JS, Supabase & Payments.” YouTube, 23 May 2025, www.youtube.com/watch?v=XUkNR-JfHwo&t=13719s.

Theme 03

Backend, Realtime, and Security

I first studied WebSockets and Redis while building a small realtime leaderboard project to learn how to implement that feature in my project. After reviewing the project requirements, I realized the leaderboard did not need instant updates, so Redis was not used in the final app. However, the experiment gave me a foundation for understanding live data. I later applied that learning through Supabase Realtime for focus request updates, while the remaining sources guided the app’s authentication, PostgreSQL data, Row Level Security.

Theme 04

Problem Research and User Needs

I used these studies to understand the screen time problem across the app’s main audiences of students, teens,and working adults. The research connected high screen use and frequent interruptions with sleep, routines, well-being, family conflict, and perceived productivity. It helped me frame my app as an accountability and balance tool that protects focus sessions.

  • Amuthan, Shwetha, et al. “Screen Time and Its Determinants among Adolescents in Chennai: A Cross-Sectional Study.” Journal of Family Medicine and Primary Care, vol. 15, no. 3, 2026, pp. 1160–64. doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_442_25.

  • Anderson, Monica, Michelle Faverio, and Eugenie Park. “How Teens and Parents Approach Screen Time.” Pew Research Center, 11 Mar. 2024, www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/03/11/how-teens-and-parents-approach-screen-time/.

  • Bondanini, Giorgia, et al. “The Dual Impact of Digital Connectivity: Balancing Productivity and Well-Being in the Modern Workplace.” International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, vol. 22, no. 6, 2025, article 845. doi.org/10.3390/ijerph22060845.

  • Duke, Éilish, and Christian Montag. “Smartphone Addiction, Daily Interruptions and Self-Reported Productivity.” Addictive Behaviors Reports, vol. 6, 2017, pp. 90–95. doi.org/10.1016/j.abrep.2017.07.002.
